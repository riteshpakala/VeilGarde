How does the flywheel grow the heads?

On the regime that keeps the detector current as generators change, without ever keeping an image.

Generators change every month, and a detector trained once falls behind. The answer is a flywheel. Find where the base is wrong, fetch images of that kind, and train candidates on them. Promote only what passes a battery of images the detector must never get wrong.

VeilGarde runs the same loop with one change at its centre. It stores the features a head needs, never the images they came from.

A store of features

Every image becomes one row of six columns: the two DINOv2 embeddings, the nine capture values, the warp score, the three sharpness values and the noiseprint. That is everything a head, the fusion head or the combiner trains on. The image is read, measured in memory and dropped.

The format is the research store's, so its 138,247 rows in 59 shards read as they are. VeilGarde's own rows add provenance: the source and its URL, the licence, the native size, a difference hash, the router's scores and the warp model that measured them.

Aiming at the blind spots

The gap miner reads the store and scores the live models on it, bucket by bucket: by domain, camera or processed, by category and by label. A bucket's gap is how often the base is wrong plus how often it disagrees with the pooled verdict.

On the July cache it found the research's two worst buckets, in the research's order: processed generated photographs at a gap of 0.33 and camera-domain ones at 0.27. Photoreal generated images are still the blind spot, and the miner writes the jobs that fill it.

Fetching without keeping

Sixteen sources answer the jobs, from Wikimedia and the Met to Civitai and Bluesky's firehose, each with the Python collectors' retry rules and a cursor that resumes. Every fetched image passes a gate before it is measured. It must decode, measure at least 512 pixels on its short side and weigh at least 20 KB. It must also be new: not the same bytes as a stored row, and not a near copy by its difference hash.

Generated images are then routed by CLIP, the argmax of their embedding against a fixed matrix of category prompts. A row lands in the shard, and the bytes are gone.

On its first run against two keyless sources it fetched 29 images, refused 9 that the seed store already held, and kept 20 rows in 62 seconds. No image was written.

Weekly candidates

Once a week the flywheel trains on top of live: the four atlas heads, and the base warm-started with 80 trees. Each candidate is a complete bundle, measured beside live, with a review file that says which slots to promote and why. On 2026-10-19 the candidate raised the store's base-and-pooled agreement from 0.9857 to 0.9894.

The battery

The battery is a set of images grouped by what the detector must do with them. Six groups are hard: every image in them must keep its verdict. Two more are ratchets that must not fall below their recorded floors.

Version 2 holds eight groups over 100 images, copied into the work area and checked byte for byte. Version 1 had twelve more, built on atlas anchor images that no longer exist; they found nothing and failed every full gate, so the owner retired them. A new atlas will be built later. The eight groups kept v1's floors, unchanged.

The full gate now passes on live: 100 images, every group at its floor, in 50 seconds. On v1's promote gate the verdicts equalled the Python runner's on 50 of 50 images, in 20 seconds against its 4 minutes 3 seconds.

Promote is the only writer of live

Promote takes one slot from a candidate into live. It refuses a frozen slot outright. It refuses a base that does not keep live's trees byte for byte, a calibration not bound to live's sensor model, and an atlas head that fails admission.

Then it snapshots live, writes the slot, re-pins the manifest and runs the battery. On a failure it restores the snapshot.

It earned its place on the first day. The weekly base's 80 trees flipped IMG_0291.JPG, a knife-edge image the battery holds, from 0.757 to 0.184, and promote refused it and restored live. So did a 60-tree start. A 20-tree start kept the image at 0.613 and promoted.

What is still open

The atlas groups are retired, not replaced. Until a new atlas is built, the art, blur, human and CGI heads are gated only by the eight groups that remain and by admission.
