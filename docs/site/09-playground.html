How do I run each stage myself?

On the playground that walks both lanes stage by stage, from the capture signature to a signature read off the warp residual.

Each chapter before this one explains a part. The playground runs them, one after another, on this Mac, and checks every step against something it must equal. It is one program, read top to bottom, and it exits with 0 only when every checkpoint holds.

Playground/run.sh

It writes nothing outside its own scratch folder, Playground/.work, and only from the training step on.

The detector, one part at a time

Steps 0 and 1 load the Metal library, verify the shipped bundle against its manifest, and pick the images. They are the two parity crops with VeilGarde's own goldens, and a dozen final_mixed images when the T9 is mounted.

Step 2 measures whether a sensor made the first crop: its capture signature at native resolution, and the screenshot gate. Step 3 reads what it depicts, through DINOv2's two views of one image, the camera head's plain crop and the heads' forced resize.

Step 4 builds the camera head's 393 columns by hand, the embedding and then the capture values, and asks the head for P(AI). A checkpoint holds that number to the camera member's score in the full detector's stack.

Step 5 runs the full detector and prints every member of its stack and every revision. When the capture signature is absent and the gate is quiet, the combiner revision's sum is worked out term by term around the camera member, and a checkpoint holds it to the revision's own score. Both crops' results are then held to VeilGarde's goldens, every label exact and every number within 1e-4.

Training, in miniature

Step 6 writes a recipe in Swift: a two-tree warm start on the shipped camera head, the human head, and the combiner, three trainable members. It prints the plan before anything trains, then runs the engine into a candidate bundle.

The rows come from a seeded sample of the work area's feature store, copied and never written back. Without that store, seeded synthetic rows stand in, and the step says so.

A toy fit on a few hundred rows means little, so the step proves the machinery instead. The warm start keeps the shipped head's trees byte for byte and adds two. Frigate's evaluator reads the candidate's head with predictions identical to VeilGarde's own. The candidate verifies, every hash against its manifest.

The flywheel, and the watermark

Step 7 copies the shipped bundle as a live bundle and builds a small battery: every image must keep the call the shipped detector gives it today. Promote refuses a frozen weight and a slot that no longer exists. The human head from step 6 goes through admission and the battery, and is rolled back. The checkpoints say the weights never moved and live is byte for byte what it was.

Step 8 is the other lane. It signs the first crop with its own fragility pattern, read off the warp residual, and matches it against itself. It then reports a half-area crop and a JPEG re-encode; whether the signature holds on real crops is the acceptance run's question, chapter 8's.

For engineers: where each piece lives
  • The playground is a package of its own, Playground/Package.swift, so building it never touches VeilGarde's targets; run.sh builds it in release and installs the Metal library.
  • scripts/cli.sh route <image> --bundle Playground/.work/candidates/playground runs the candidate it trains; scripts/cli.sh flywheel status --work-area Playground/.work shows its live bundle.
  • Playground/README.md lists every checkpoint and what it compares.
