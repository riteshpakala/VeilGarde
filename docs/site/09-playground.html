How do I run each stage myself?

On the playground that walks both regimes stage by stage, from the warp model alone to the stacked heads.

Each chapter before this one explains a layer. The playground runs them, one after another, on this Mac, and checks every step against something it must equal. It is one program, read top to bottom, and it exits with 0 only when every checkpoint holds.

Playground/run.sh

It writes nothing outside its own scratch folder, Playground/.work, and only from the training step on.

The inference regime, one part at a time

The first six steps take one image through the detector the way chapters 1 to 5 tell it. The lens cuts it into 32-pixel tiles and prints the centre tile's 19 features by name. The base warp model alone then scores every tile. The camera-sensor score is worked out in the open: the tile vote, the two cuts that call it, and the trust the call deserves.

The lens's whole result is held against Garde's, leaf for leaf but for the sensor fields the two cuts changed. Then come the capture signature, the 397 numbers the fusion head reads, and the full detector. The combiner's arithmetic is printed term by term and checked against the router's own score.

The training regime, in miniature

Step 7 writes a recipe in Swift. It names a toy warp tile model of 20 trees, a two-tree warm start on the shipped fusion head, the human head, and the combiner. It prints the plan before anything trains, then runs the engine into a candidate bundle.

A toy separates its own images perfectly, so its numbers mean little. What the step proves is the machinery. The toy model reads back through Frigate's evaluator with identical predictions. A calibration binds to the toy model's bytes and refuses the shipped model's, and the warm start keeps the shipped head's 940 trees byte for byte.

The flywheel, and the watermark

Step 8 copies the shipped bundle as a live bundle and builds a small battery: every image that must keep the call the shipped detector gives it today. Promote refuses the frozen warp tile model. The human head from step 7 goes through admission and the battery, and is rolled back. The checkpoints say the frozen artifacts never moved and live is byte for byte what it was.

Step 9 signs the first image with its own fragility pattern and matches it against itself, then reports a half-area crop and a JPEG re-encode. Whether v1 holds on real crops is the acceptance run's question, chapter 8's.

What it showed on 2026-10-19

The run took under two minutes in release, its build included, and held all 12 checkpoints. Step 3 worked out the first crop's camera-sensor score in the open:

tile vote: 97 of 256 tiles above 0.5 → the camera-sensor score 0.3789
two cuts on the vote: real at or under 0.7169, generated at or over 0.8365, inconclusive between
→ real, trust low

The trust is low because a 512-pixel crop carries no capture signature. On the final_mixed sample the six iPhone originals read real, five of them with high trust, and the six generated images read real too, at low trust. The sensor score is a camera detector, and the stack after it catches the generators.

On the same crop the fusion base read 0.9392 and the pooled verdict 0.9999, the combiner's sum done by hand equal to the router's. In step 9 a half-area crop matched its source at 0.789 over 379 bits, and a JPEG of it at 0.894.

For engineers: where each piece lives
  • The playground is a package of its own, Playground/Package.swift, so building it never touches VeilGarde's targets; run.sh builds it in release and installs the Metal library.
  • scripts/cli.sh route <image> --bundle Playground/.work/candidates/playground runs the candidate it trains; scripts/cli.sh flywheel status --work-area Playground/.work shows its live bundle.
  • Playground/README.md lists every checkpoint and what it compares.
