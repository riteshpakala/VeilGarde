How does the fusion base weigh the evidence?

On the camera base expert: the warp score, what the image depicts, the capture signature and sharpness, read by one booster.

The warp lens asks whether a sensor made the pixels. That question has a ceiling, because a resized photograph and a render both lose the sensor's trace. The fusion base asks a wider question with the lens's answer as one of its inputs.

It reads four things at once: the lens's weighted score, what the image depicts, the capture signature and how sharp the image is. One booster weighs them together, and its probability is called the base.

One row of 397 numbers

Every image becomes one row. The first number is the warp lens's weighted score. The next 384 are DINOv2-small's embedding of the whole image, resized to 256 and centre-cropped to 224. Nine more are the capture signature, and the last three measure sharpness, at the salient region and across the image.

The order is the booster's, and it never changes. A model trained on one order and run on another would read the capture peak where it expects a sharpness value.

What the image depicts

DINOv2 was trained to describe images, not to judge them. Its embedding places a portrait near other portraits and a watercolour near other watercolours. That is the axis the lens cannot see: a generator's typical subjects and styles.

The embedding alone is no detector. The v2.0 probe found every semantic and physical feature tied to the data it was trained on, with no feature that holds across generators it has not seen. The fusion works because each axis covers another's blind spot.

The booster

The fusion head is an ensemble of 940 trees of depth 5 over the 397 columns. Its first 900 trees are the frozen reference's, byte for byte, and the last 40 were added by a warm start. The flywheel moves the base only that way.

Its probability is called at 0.5 with an open band of 0.04. That operating point stays apart from the lens's 0.56 on purpose. The two models were calibrated on different data, and unifying them would move one off its own curve.

When the flat-UI gate fires, the screenshot head takes the verdict instead. A screenshot is real but not a capture, and the camera-anchored base would read it as generated.

When the capture signature is absent

An image whose capture signature is present carries the sensor's own evidence, and the specialist heads are suppressed for it. An image without one is where the base matters most.

For those images the base is the verdict. The AI-video campaign measured it on 2026-07-11: the combiner weighs the base's logit at 3.82, and the art and blur heads carry no generated weight at all. The heads can move a capture-absent verdict only within the combiner's caps. Chapter 4 shows that arithmetic.

The frozen reference

The 900-tree reference never takes the verdict. Two mechanisms ask it questions, because they were calibrated against it, and a live base that moved would silently move their operating points.

The preview tail asks it about a small image with its capture shortcut neutralized. A sub-512 image has no valid capture signature, and in the 2026-07-17 data 85.5% of such rows were generated, so the base learned that small means generated. Neutralizing marks the capture valid and its native size at least 576, and asks again.

How the base moves

A booster trained from scratch on new data does not reproduce the old one's boundary. The research learned this five times over: every from-scratch store retrain failed the battery, and warm starts passed.

So the base grows by adding trees. VeilGarde's warm start keeps the parent's 940 trees byte for byte, adds 80 at a learning rate of 0.03, and took 38 seconds on the seed store's 113,176 rows. On the 1,569 new-generator rows it held out, recall rose from 0.9955 to 0.9975.

The battery then decides. Those 80 trees flipped IMG_0291.JPG, the knife-edge image the baseline audit names, from 0.757 to 0.184, and the promote was refused. A 20-tree start kept it at 0.613 and passed.
