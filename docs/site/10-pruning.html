What could VeilGarde do without?

On a study that takes each part out, measures what the detector loses on held-out data, and asks the battery before it believes the answer.

Every part of the detector was added for a reason, often after a measurement on data that has since moved. The study asks the question again for each one: take it out, and what does the detector lose?

The rules came first. A part is within tolerance when taking it out costs at most 0.005 AUC on held-out data and moves at most 0.5% of the calls. Every difference carries a 95% interval from 1,000 resamples, so a difference too small to measure says so.

One control was added after the first run, and the rules stayed as written. A model refit with only its seed changed already moves some calls, so the study refits twice more with other seeds. That gives the noise floor every other refit is read against.

The weight map earns its place

The weighted score weighs each tile by where it sits, its texture and its confidence, then blends toward the confident tiles. A plain mean of the same tile probabilities loses 0.021 AUC, the median 0.030, the tile vote 0.040. The arithmetic is worth keeping.

The calibration is two cuts

The camera-sensor score maps the tile vote through Platt scaling, then calls it against a threshold with a band. Platt is monotone, so the call it makes is a cut on the vote itself.

On all 1,738 test images the calibrated call equals two cuts on the raw vote: real at or under 71.7% of tiles, generated at or over 83.7%, inconclusive between. The coefficient, the intercept and the threshold matter for the probability the result reported, not for the call.

Found over every vote a float32 can hold, the exact cuts are 0.71687 and 0.83654, and the shipped contract is now those two cuts. Its call is the Platt contract's on every vote, and the camera-sensor score the result reports is the vote itself.

The router, on real images

The census runs the full detector over the battery's 100 images and 200 final_mixed images and counts what each part did.

The stack moved the fusion base's call on 6 of the 252 images with no capture signature: once to the right call, five times to the wrong one. The frame seam ran on 3 images and decided one, IMG_0500, a hard anchor of the battery. The preview tail fired once, on a hard anchor's preview. Both are rare and both are load-bearing.

Zoom escalation, the x-domain gate and the render taxonomy never ran: each is off by default. Their code and the x-domain centroids are dormant, kept for a campaign that turns them on.

The majority route's core

The owner asked on 2026-10-20 which of the 19 features the majority route needs. The question starts from the trio of FR, LV and CV, the warp residual's fragility rate, local variance and direction coherence, and asks what each other feature adds on top. The core study answers it by refitting the warp model on a mask of features.

The mask lets the trees split only on the features it names, and the model stays 19 columns wide. So every arm would deploy and calibrate as the shipped model does, and a masked fit grows the same trees as a refit on those columns alone, node for node. Each arm gets its own camera-sensor cuts from the validation images, by the engine's rule, and is scored on the 1,738 test images.

The rules came first, in the code and the ledger. A feature moves the needle when the tile-vote AUC it adds to the trio has an interval that excludes zero and a gain above the seed floor, 0.0032. The golden combination is the smallest arm within 0.005 vote AUC of all 19. Its iPhone originals may read real no more than 2 points less often, and its generated images no more than 2 points more often. It must hold twice: in the stem split, and in a second run that leaves all 438 iPhone originals out of training and scores them.

The trio alone is not the core. It loses 0.127 vote AUC against all 19 and calls 67% of the generated test images real. Every raw-tile feature adds to it: noise_ratio most, then the three cross-channel correlations. Of the other residual features, CM and CR add, CC adds nothing measurable, and each seam adds about 0.005.

The nine raw-tile features alone, with no warp at all, beat the ten residual features, 0.739 against 0.684. Most of final_mixed's signal is not in the round trip. The round trip still counts: the trio and the raw nine together reach 0.767, well past the raw nine alone.

The golden combination is the trio and the raw nine, twelve features. It is 0.002 under all 19 in the stem split and 0.0005 over it with the iPhone pool left out, both intervals holding zero. It reads more iPhone originals real in both runs. It leaves out CM, CC, CR and the four seams. Forward selection from the trio stopped at seven features in both runs, 0.011 and 0.004 short, and in the second run those seven called too many generated images real.

The deployable candidate, core-trio-raw9, is the study's arm byte for byte, the same SHA-256. Its own cuts are real at or under 61.7% of tiles and generated at or over 70.1%.

The second run says what the first could not. With no iPhone photograph in training, all 19 features call 53% of the 438 originals real, against 92% of the 74 held out in the stem split. Much of the camera call on iPhone photographs is learned from iPhone photographs.

Is the rest already in the residual?

The owner's second question: can what the noiseprint and the fusion head know be found by expanding the trio? The redundancy check asks it of five residual views, from the trio to all 19. A view is a warp model's weighted score, its tile vote, its three sharpness values, and the mean and spread of each feature it reads.

It runs on the 8,760 final_mixed images the store holds, their DINOv2 and capture rows joined by path and a noiseprint measured into a scratch store. Every second-stage model is cross-fitted on the 2,620 held-out images, so none sees a warp score from an image the warp model trained on. The rules came first. A signal is redundant when it adds at most 0.005 AUC on top of a view. A view reconstructs a signal when it predicts that signal's call at an AUC of at least 0.90.

No view reconstructs either call; the best stops at 0.766 and 0.872. On top of every view both signals add more than the bar, each interval above zero. So neither is already in the residual, however the trio is expanded.

The two signals are not alike. Once DINOv2 and capture are present, the noiseprint adds nothing measurable in any view, between −0.004 and +0.003. The residual is the other way round: on top of DINOv2, capture and the noiseprint together it still adds 0.085 to 0.114. Replayed through the shipped combiner on 2,288 capture-absent images, the noiseprint term moves between 4 and 13 calls, none of them to the right label.

On the 121 held-out iPhone originals the golden set's sensor call and the noiseprint agree. The sensor reads 116 real, the noiseprint 117 camera, and 112 are both.

One model for every signal

The owner also asked whether one condensed model could hold what the fusion head, DINOv2 and the noiseprint hold, beside the warp core. It is a small network trained in Swift on MLX. A shared network reads each tile's core features, its texture and its place. The tiles pool by their mean, a soft tile vote and a texture-weighted vote. The capture values with the noiseprint, and DINOv2's embedding, enter beside them.

The research left two warnings, and the rules carry both. A learned meta-combiner once raised real false positives from 8% to 50 to 92%. So an arm must hold its real false-positive rate within 0.01 of a boosted model on the same inputs, at that model's recall. An attention-pooled head once fired on single regions, so the pooling is by means. An arm must also match the boosted model's AUC within 0.005, call every HARD battery image right offline, and read at least as many iPhone originals real as the trio warp model.

The fusion head and the noiseprint, in the full detector

The last question asks whether the fusion head and the noiseprint are still needed once the specialist heads sit on top. The matrix runs the whole detector eight ways: the shipped warp model or the golden one, the fusion head or the warp lens alone as the camera base, with or without the noiseprint. Each way runs the full battery, the census's 300 images and the 438 iPhone originals.

The warp lens alone hands the stack its own weighted verdict where the fusion head's would go, and keeps the sensor lane byte for byte. The rules came first, and the battery decides. A part is not necessary only when the battery passes without it, the 200 labelled final_mixed images lose at most 2 correct calls, and no more calls move to the wrong label than to the right one. The golden warp holds up only if its camera-sensor call agrees with the shipped model's on 98% of the images.

The fusion head is necessary. Without it the battery fails in six of its eight groups: the generated semantic anchors fall to 5 of 24 and the native cameras to 21 of 24. On the census 25 fewer final_mixed images come out right, and 64 of the 69 calls that move go the wrong way. When an iPhone original carries a capture signature the stack stands down, so its verdict is the warp lens's own, and 70 of the 438 land inconclusive.

The noiseprint is not necessary beside the shipped warp model: no call moves on any of the 738 images, and the battery passes. Beside the golden model it is. One of the user's own photographs, HNT6bs5WMAA_dU5_full_res.jpeg, reads real at 0.390 with it and generated at 0.553 without it.

The golden warp passes the battery and keeps the iPhone originals real, but by the rule written before the run it does not hold up: its camera-sensor call agrees with the shipped model's on 81.9% of the images. Where the two differ on labelled census images, the golden call is right 70 times and the shipped call 21. Most are generated images it now calls generated. Both models trained on final_mixed, so that difference is partly in-sample.

What the owner decides next

Nothing shipped changed in the 2026-10-20 study. It leaves four questions in the ledger, question-13 to question-16:

  • whether the golden twelve replace the shipped warp model and its cuts, which the rules alone do not settle;
  • whether the noiseprint term leaves the combiner while the shipped warp model ships;
  • whether to pursue the condensed model;
  • whether the site keeps its larger data cap.

What the owner took

Nothing shipped changed in the study's own run. The rules allowed five changes, listed in the ledger as question-4, and on 2026-10-19 the owner chose among them:

  • the warp model keeps its four seam features, for now, though they cost an FFT per seam and nothing measurable;
  • the fusion head keeps its three sharpness inputs, and a scratch head keeps its 500 trees;
  • the art-pair adjudicator is out of the combiner, and its head out of the bundle;
  • the camera-sensor call is two cuts on the tile vote.

Both changes are the variants the study measured, nothing refitted: the shipped combiner's weights are Garde's, and the cuts are where Garde's Platt contract turns. The full battery passes with both.

Not candidates: the warp model's size and its demosaic, noise and residual features; capture and DINOv2 in the fusion; the art and blur heads; the rescue; the frame seam and the preview tail.

For engineers: rerun the study
  • veilgarde research prune runs all three parts on the work area's tile cache and store, in about eight minutes in release, and writes research/prune-<stamp>.json; --parts picks some.
  • veilgarde research census runs the full detector over the battery and a final_mixed sample, about two minutes on the GPU.
  • A variant for the battery is the shipped bundle with one combiner field changed and re-pinned; flywheel battery --gate full --bundle <it> runs it without touching live.
  • The pruning trail walks the study's code; the research tests run it end to end on a synthetic work area.
  • veilgarde research core refits the warp model on every pre-registered feature mask in about thirteen minutes on the CPU; --leave-pool-out iphone is the second run and --combine judges the two together. train warp --features makes the deployable model.
  • veilgarde research redundancy needs the noiseprint of final_mixed's store rows in a scratch store, made once by features extract --columns noiseprint; it then takes under three minutes on the CPU.
  • veilgarde research condensed trains the condensed arms on MLX in about two minutes, after the battery's tiles and features are cut into the work area.
  • bundle derive makes a variant with one combiner edit, recorded; --base warp on the battery and the census runs the detector with the warp lens where the fusion head goes; research compare judges a variant from its census and battery report.
