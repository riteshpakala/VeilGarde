What do the specialist heads add?

On the families the base misses, the heads that read them, and the pooling that keeps a head from overturning a confident base.

The fusion base of chapter 1 is strong where it was trained. It is weaker on AI art against human art, on generative blur against a camera's, on photoreal people against real portraits. Each of those families gets a specialist: a head that knows one question well.

A head never replaces the base. It adds evidence in one direction, and a combiner weighs that evidence against the base under caps it cannot exceed.

A head is a line in DINOv2's space

Every specialist is the same primitive: a standardized linear probe on a 384-value DINOv2 embedding. It subtracts each value's mean, divides by its spread, takes a weighted sum and a sigmoid. A new specialist is a new head file, not new code.

The heads read a different embedding from the fusion head's. The image is force-resized to 360 by 640 before DINOv2 sees it, so a portrait and a landscape are read at the same shape. Every head was trained on that embedding, and the store keeps it as its own column.

The combiner's arithmetic

For an image with no capture signature, the combiner pools the base and the three atlas priors, art, blur and human, into one probability. It is asymmetric: generated evidence adds, and real evidence subtracts only while the base is unsure.

The base's logit is capped at 4.0 and each head's at 2.0. Each head adds its weight times its logit, and only when that logit is positive: a head that reads real adds nothing.

While the base sits under 0.75, one real-side term subtracts. When every head reads real, the rescue pulls by 1.0 times the weakest of their real logits, so it is only as strong as the least sure head.

The weights make the base dominant. It enters at 1.6641 and the intercept is −1.0216.

All three priors carry generated weight: art 0.1533, blur 0.2198, human 0.2999. They also decide whether the rescue fires.

The golden crop crop_ben shows the rescue at work. The base read it at 0.630, every head read it real and the base sat under the gate, so the rescue pulled the pooled probability to 0.106.

The combiner refit for two lanes

VeilGarde refit the combiner on its own store when the detector became two lanes, over the new base. It has five inputs: the base, the three generated-evidence weights and the rescue. A bounded fit keeps every weight at or above zero, so no evidence can be counted backwards.

The rescue weight is chosen by a sweep against seven protect-real probes. No candidate kept the top probe under 0.42, so the rescue took the trainer's fallback.

Why a head cannot flip a confident base

Stacking with caps is a measured choice. The research tried a monolithic combiner that blended every signal, and real false positives rose from 8% to between 50% and 92%.

The stack holds them at 0%, and the caps are the reason. A head at its cap adds at most twice its weight, under 0.6 for every head. A confident base at its cap adds four times its own, over 6.6.

When a user's own image slips past the base, the fix the research found is denser data for both, not a stronger override.

Admission

A head that blocks real images is worse than no head. Before an atlas head is promoted, it is scored on the golden reals outside Bluesky, and it must block at most 10% of them at 0.8.

A dark frame hiding the content

A fake film border can drag the pooled evidence toward real while the content inside is generated. When the verdict is real and the image wears a uniform dark frame, the frame seam runs the whole stack again on the interior.

It takes the interior's evidence only when it is decisive: at least 0.70, and at least 0.20 above the framed verdict.

The semantic-tile map

The semtile head reads each of DINOv2's 256 patch tokens and writes a 16 by 16 map of P(AI). The share of tokens above 0.5 is the semantic majority, and the map goes into the result.

The map is a vote, not a verdict. A probe on it revises a shadow state, and only the production profile commits that revision.

The preview tail

An image under 512 pixels on its short side has no valid capture signature, and the base learned that small means generated. Quoted from the research's record, 85.5% of the invalid-capture rows it measured were generated.

So a small image read as generated, with a clean semantic vote, flips to real only when two reads agree. The preview head must read it at 0.45 or under, and the fusion head, asked again with its capture flags neutralized, at 0.30 or under.

Until the two lanes, the second read came from a frozen copy of the original fusion head. That copy read the warp score, so it left the bundle, and the tail now asks the live head. Its cuts were set against the old head and are kept as they were; every result reports whether the tail ran, fired and decided.
