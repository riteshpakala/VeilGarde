How does the router pick a path?

On the one entry point of the full detector, and the order in which its evidence compounds.

The router is where the layers meet. It picks the image's domain, runs that domain's base expert, then lets a short list of high-precision steps revise the verdict, always in the same order. Every step is one file, and the ones that are not live sit behind a flag.

Two domains

A photograph and a screenshot need different experts. The router reads the image's signals once, then picks the screenshot domain when the flat-UI gate fires and the camera domain otherwise. A caller's hint overrides the gate.

Routing is never by image size. A small image is still a camera image, and the preview tail handles what size does to the base.

The camera domain's expert is the warp lens and the fusion head of chapter 3. The screenshot domain's expert is a content head trained on screenshots alone.

The order of the steps

The order is fixed because each step reads what the one before it wrote.

The verdict is frozen before the semantic chain as the pre-semantic state, and kept in the diagnostics. That is what makes a route replayable: the chain's revisions can be read against what came before them.

Stacking, not blending

Each step either keeps the verdict or revises it on decisive evidence, and each gate was measured before it went live. The frame seam takes the interior's verdict only when it reads at least 0.70 and sits at least 0.20 above the outer score. The preview tail flips only under three agreeing reads.

The cost of a gate is a step that rarely fires. The alternative, one model that blends every signal, raised real false positives from 8% to as much as 92% in the research's measurement.

What the result keeps

The result carries two numbers, and they answer different questions. The majority is the camera-sensor anchor of chapter 1, untouched by the stack. The weighted score is the compounded verdict after every step.

Since schema v2 the semantic majority never owns the public majority. A reader who wants to know whether a camera made the pixels reads one field, and a reader who wants the detector's whole judgement reads the other.

The JSON is Garde's schema-v2 bytes, written with sorted keys. VeilGarde's route equals Garde's byte for byte on 37 images with Garde's Metal library, and within 1.04e-5 with Frigate's. The difference is MLX's kernels: on 36 of the 37 images the largest one sits in the semantic tiles.
