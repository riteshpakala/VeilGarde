What do the specialist heads add?

On the families the base misses, the camera evidence the noiseprint adds, and the pooling that keeps both honest.

The fusion base is strong where it was trained. It is weaker on AI art against human art, on generative blur against a camera's, on photoreal people against real portraits. Each of those families gets a specialist: a head that knows one question well.

A head never replaces the base. It adds evidence in one direction, and a combiner weighs that evidence against the base under caps it cannot exceed.

A head is a line in DINOv2's space

Every specialist is the same primitive: a standardized linear probe on a 384-value DINOv2 embedding. It subtracts each value's mean, divides by its spread, takes a weighted sum and a sigmoid. A new specialist is a new head file, not new code.

The heads read a different embedding from the fusion's. The image is force-resized to 360 by 640 before DINOv2 sees it, so a portrait and a landscape are read at the same shape. Every head was trained on that embedding, and the store keeps it as its own column.

The noiseprint's camera evidence

The capture signature reads the sensor at native resolution, and a single recompression destroys it. The noiseprint was built for that zone. It is a small CNN whose first layer is constrained to predict each pixel from its neighbours, so it reads the residual of the content rather than the content.

It scores a 4×4 grid of 128-pixel gray patches, and the mean sigmoid is P(camera). The v202 research measured it on held-out images at 0.941 AUC at native quality and 0.906 at WebP 75. Its low side includes laundered web reals, so it only ever subtracts generated evidence; it never adds any.

The combiner's arithmetic

For an image with no capture signature, the combiner pools the base, the three atlas priors and the noiseprint into one probability. It is asymmetric: generated evidence adds, and real evidence subtracts only while the base is unsure.

The base's logit is capped at 4.0 and each head's at 2.0. Each head adds its weight times its logit, only when that logit is positive.

Garde's combiner first let an art-pair head pull the art logit down whenever it read the image as real art. The pruning study found that adjudicator broke 19 of the store's calls to fix 10, with every battery call held without it, so the shipped combiner no longer asks for it, and its head left the bundle. A combiner file that asks for the adjudication still gets it.

While the base sits under 0.75, two real-side terms subtract. When every head reads real, the rescue pulls by 0.5 times the weakest of their real logits, so it is only as strong as the least sure head. The noiseprint subtracts 0.8 times its camera logit.

The shipped weights make the base dominant. It enters at 3.8205, the intercept is −0.6755, and of the three priors only human carries generated weight, 0.1967. Art and blur still count: they decide whether the rescue fires.

Why a head cannot flip a confident base

Stacking with caps is a measured choice. The research tried a monolithic combiner that blended every signal, and real false positives rose from 8% to between 50% and 92%. The stack holds them at 0%.

The caps are the reason. A head at its cap adds at most twice its weight to the logit, and the base enters at almost four times its own. When a user's own image slips past the base, the fix the research found is denser data for both, not a stronger override.

Admission

A head that blocks real images is worse than no head. Before an atlas head is promoted, it is scored on the golden reals outside Bluesky, and it must block at most 10% of them at 0.8.

Retrained from the seed store on 2026-10-19, every atlas head was admitted. The art head blocked 1.6% against the shipped head's 4.7%, blur 6.2% against 9.4%, human and cgi none.
