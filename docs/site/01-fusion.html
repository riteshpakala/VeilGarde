How does the fusion head read an image?

On the camera base expert: what the image depicts and how it was captured, in one row, read by one booster.

The fusion head gives the detector its first answer for a camera image. It reads two kinds of evidence at once: what the image depicts, from a frozen DINOv2 embedding, and whether a sensor made its pixels, from the capture signature.

One booster weighs the two together, and its probability is called the base. Every other part of the detector builds on that number. Chapter 2 shows the specialist heads that add to it, and chapter 3 the order in which they do.

One row of 393 numbers

Every image becomes one row. The first numbers are DINOv2-small's embedding of the whole image, its short edge resized to 256 and its centre cropped to 224. The last nine are the capture signature, measured on the native pixels.

The order is the booster's and the store's, and it never changes. A model trained on one order and run on another would read the capture peak where it expects an embedding value. The head the bundle ships reads 393 columns.

Three rows a head can read

A fusion head's width names its row, so a bundle of any of them runs. The shipped head reads the first; the other two were trained and measured, and none passed the battery yet.

What the image depicts

DINOv2 was trained to describe images, not to judge them. Its embedding places a portrait near other portraits and a watercolour near other watercolours. That is an axis no measurement of a camera can see: a generator's typical subjects and styles.

The embedding alone is no detector. The research's v2.0 probe, quoted here, found every semantic and physical feature tied to the data it was trained on. No feature held across generators it had not seen. The fusion works because each axis covers the other's blind spot.

Whether a sensor made the pixels

The capture signature is measured, not learned. A camera's sensor and its demosaicing leave a periodic trace in the highest frequencies of the native pixels. A generator's output does not carry it, and a resize destroys it.

The lens reads a centre crop at native resolution, takes its high-pass residual and finds the peak of that residual's spectrum. A peak of 30 or more counts as present. The short side must reach 512 pixels, or the image is marked invalid, because the peak is not reliable below that size.

All nine values enter the row as they are. A present signature does more than inform the head: it also silences every generated-side specialist, which chapter 2 shows.

The booster

The fusion head is an ensemble of 500 trees of depth 5 over the row. VeilGarde trained it from scratch on its own feature store, and chapter 5 shows that fit.

Its probability is called at 0.5 with an open band of 0.04 either side. A base inside the band is inconclusive, above it generated, below it real.

When the flat-UI gate fires, the screenshot head takes the verdict instead. A screenshot is real but not a capture, and a head trained on camera rows would read it as generated.

When the capture signature is absent

An image whose capture signature is present carries the sensor's own evidence, and the specialist heads are suppressed for it. An image without one is where the base matters most.

For those images a combiner pools the base with the specialist heads, and the base enters with the largest weight, 1.6641.

Its logit is capped at 4.0, a head's at 2.0, so a saturated head cannot overturn a confident base. The next chapter shows that arithmetic.

How the base moves

A booster trained from scratch on new data does not reproduce the old one's boundary, so it can lose cases the old one got right. The research learned this five times over: every from-scratch store retrain failed the battery, and warm starts passed.

So the flywheel moves the base only by a warm start. It keeps the live head's trees byte for byte and adds new ones fitted to what they still get wrong, 80 trees at a learning rate of 0.03 by default. Promote refuses a base that does not keep the live head's trees.

The shipped head is the exception, and the reason is its width. The head before it read 397 columns, and a warm start cannot continue a model on columns it never had. So it was trained from scratch, with an evaluation twin to measure it on rows it never saw.

Under the warp era the battery decided what a warm start may add. Measured on the warp era's head, a start of 80 trees flipped IMG_0291, the knife-edge flux anchor, to real. The promote was refused, and a start of 20 trees kept it generated and passed.

The new head calls IMG_0291 real. It is one of the four hard battery images the two-lane detector misses, so no base reaches live until a warm start calls them right.
