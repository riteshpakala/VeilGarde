How does the warp residual read a clip?

On the spectrogram as the picture the watermark signs, the way back to sound, and the acceptance that decided v1 on audio.

The watermark reads pictures, so a sound is first drawn as one. A spectrogram puts time left to right and pitch bottom to top, and each pixel says how loud one band is at one moment. That picture goes to the image watermark unchanged: the same sixteen warps, the same fragility rate, the same four bits a tile. Nothing is written into the sound.

The owner asked for it on 2026-11-08 to test whether the paradigm crosses domains. Behind it is a theory: a generated sound, drawn this way, may show the warp residual the same kind of trace a generated image shows. This chapter covers the first half. It sets out the medium and the mapping, then asks whether a signature read off them survives what happens to sound.

A clip becomes a picture

A clip is mixed to one channel and resampled to its profile's rate. Two profiles were fixed before any number was measured, one for music and one for speech, and the owner means to merge them once the research says how.

Each frame is a stretch of the clip under a periodic Hann window, and the frames step one hop apart, with half a frame of zeros added at each end. Zeros rather than a mirrored edge, so a frame holds only the samples inside it. A cut of the clip then draws the same columns as its source, everywhere but its own two ends.

The mapping onto the residual's domain

The warp residual was built for photographs, so the picture is made to look like one to it. Each choice has a reason:

  • A linear frequency axis: every tile covers the same band, so a warp moves energy between equal neighbours, as it moves pixels.
  • A fixed loudness scale, −96 to 0 dBFS in 256 levels: the same sound is the same pixel in any clip, so a cut's pixels equal its source's. A scale set by each clip's own peak would move them all.
  • Eight bits a pixel, the level in all three channels of the lens's own bitmap: the lens reads the bytes as they are.
  • A Hann window at 75% overlap: a held note draws a horizontal line, an onset a vertical one, and noise a grain, the texture the bicubic warps disturb.
  • No DC row: an offset in the sound cannot reach the picture.

The floor at −96 dBFS keeps quiet passages textured instead of flat. A floor at −80 would flatten them into tiles that agree with any other quiet tile. It is named as a fallback and was not used.

The same round trip, on sound

From the picture on, the code is the image watermark's own. Every full tile of 32 frames by 32 bins is warped through the sixteen homographies of chapter 7 and back at two strengths. Its fragility rate is how much more the stronger warp loses. Its four bits compare it with its neighbours: east is later in time, south is lower in pitch.

On a photograph the round trip loses the fine structure a camera leaves. On a spectrogram it loses the fine structure of harmonics, onsets and noise. Whether a generated sound loses it differently from a recorded one is the next question; this chapter measures the signature, not that.

The bits read off sound lean towards zero, where a photograph's are even. A tile tends to be less fragile than the one below it, which is lower in pitch. So two unrelated clips agree on more bits than two unrelated photographs do, and the music table below gives the shares.

Finding a cut in time

A cut keeps every frequency, so its picture is a band of the signed one, offset in time alone. The matcher reads the cut's grid at four phases every 8 frames, where an image needs sixteen, and slides each over the signature at every whole-tile offset. The best column, times the hop over the rate, is where the cut starts. The thresholds are the watermark's own.

A cut that starts on a hop is a shifted picture. One that starts on a tile boundary matches exactly: a test cut 96 hops into the music-like clip scores 1 at its column. A cut that starts between hops holds other samples in every frame, so its columns are near the source's but not equal. The same test's cut 87 samples past a hop scored 0.680, at the right place. That case is what the acceptance measures.

The picture back to sound

The transform has an exact inverse. Each frame goes back through the inverse FFT, is windowed again and added where frames overlap, and the sum is divided by the squared windows' sum at every sample.

From the picture alone the sound comes back too: each bin keeps its phase and takes its loudness from its level. The bars were fixed before measuring, and the second one missed.

The test it had to pass

The acceptance is the image watermark's, mirrored one to one and fixed in code before the run. Each clip is signed and matched against itself. Then three things are scored: a cut of half its length at a random sample, an AAC re-encode, and the cut against the next clip's signature. On every fourth clip the cut's speed is also changed to 0.9 and 0.75 of the original, and those are measured, not claimed.

v1 on audio dies on any of three counts:

  • the cut matches with its offset in fewer than 60% of clips;
  • the median re-encode score falls under 0.75;
  • no threshold reaches 80% cut recall with at most 5% false matches.

The fallbacks were named before the run: a phase search at quarter hops, the hop halved, the floor at −80 dB, and the image lane's own.

What the run showed

v1 on audio was killed under both profiles in the runs of 2026-11-08, for different reasons. The picture is faithful: every cut that started on a tile boundary matched, under both profiles. What fails is the cut that starts anywhere else.

Finding the time phases

The owner's next idea was the study of this phase. Slice a clip into short sections, tile each section's picture, and compare the residuals against the whole clip. Find the time step and time phase that recur. The features of a generated sound would be temporal, the step of its synthesis. The focus is speech, and music is held as observation.

The instrument reads the watermark's own grid at all 32 time phases and interleaves them. The fragility becomes a trace dense in time. Beside it sits the residual's slope per picture column, finer than a tile. Sections of half a second to two seconds are compared with the whole trace at every offset, and peaks that recur at one spacing are a recurrence. The traces' spectra are read for lines that stand above a running baseline and carry harmonics. A line no lower line divides is a time step.

Eight hypothesis rows were written before any clip was measured. Their controls have a known answer: synthetic clips with a note grid and a click train every quarter second, noise, silence and tones, and the AAC codec's frame. The instrument was replaced twice on its own unit tests before the first run. A flat picture then made it gain a floor between the first and the second pass of the controls. Each change is in the study's rules file, dated.

On speech: no synthesis step, only prosody

The 56 utterances of seven macOS voices were traced at quarter hop, a column every 4 ms. No utterance shows a step between 8 and 20 ms. Every line the scan found is the rhythm of the words, and no utterance has a recurring section. Sixteen utterances were re-encoded as AAC and traced again, and the codec frame shows nothing before or after. The table has the counts.

Music, held

The beat is the per-column residual's dominant step in every 30-second segment of the three masters. It is 436 ms for two of them and 677 ms for the third, with a spread under half a frame. On ten excerpts at quarter hop no step fell under 20 ms, and the AAC frame showed in one. The 32-phase search was not run on music before the day closed.

Read the passes narrowly. They are one synthetic corpus of clean voices, with no room, no microphone and no recorded human speech. They rest on 39 and 20 clips, so a false-match rate of 2 in 20 at 0.75 could sit anywhere from about 1% to 30%. A half cut of an utterance carries a few hundred bits. Noise, rooms, phone codecs and re-recording were not in the passing runs, and in the first chapter's run noise at 30 dB broke 33 of 39 utterances.

The next measurements, in order, each with its rule written first:

  1. The owner chooses the proposal: 32 phases is cheap and passes; quarter hops are sharper and slower.
  2. Real speech: a command that records raw sound from the default input, and recorded voices reading the same sentences.
  3. A bigger, harder corpus: hundreds of clips from many speakers, so false matches are measured on more than 40 strangers.
  4. Speech's real transforms: noise first, then rooms, a speaker-to-microphone re-recording, telephone bandwidth and codecs, speed and pitch.
  5. More bits per second of speech: more rows in the picture, or several cuts pooled, measured as a proposal.
  6. The theory: a neural voice beside the classic ones and beside recorded speech, through the time-phase study.

The research notes that direct this work are in Sources/VeilGardeAudio/README.md.

What it cannot do yet

  • A cut that starts between hops is not a shifted picture. At four phases its score falls under 0.75 often enough to kill v1 under both profiles; at 32 phases every speech cut was found, and that search is a proposal, not the default.
  • Neighbouring seconds of one track share their texture, so a music signature can pass for its neighbour's.
  • An MP3 of a 24-bit master fills the master's quiet passages with codec noise, and the master's signature misses it.
  • White noise at 30 dB and changes of speed break it, and it does not claim them.
