How does the detector reach one verdict?

On the five stages of the full detector, the stack of members that each say what they see, the revisions that read them, and the result it writes.

The detector is one declaration. It computes the image's signals once, asks every member of its stack for its own take, then runs a short list of revisions over what they said. Every member and every revision is one file.

The number that comes out keeps the anchor's meaning: the probability that the image was generated, called at 0.5.

Five stages, one after another

Detection is a chain of stages, each handing its output to the next. The change explorer walks each one as a trail of its own.

#StageWhat it doesTrail
1DecodeThe file to the pixels every model saw: NSImage first, then ImageIO.DE
2Shared SignalsComputed once: the screenshot gate, the capture reading, both DINOv2 readings and the patch tokens.SG
3StackEvery member reads the signals and says what it sees, each with its own score.SK
4RevisionsThe combiner sets the verdict around one anchor; the frame seam and the preview tail revise it only on decisive evidence.RV
5ResultThe verdict, every member's output and every revision's record, written as schema-v1 JSON.RS

The declaration is the detector. A new member is one file that says what it sees and one line in the stack; a new revision is one file and one line after it. The training recipe of chapter 5 declares the trainable members the same way.

The stack: every member speaks

A member reads only the shared signals and never another member's answer. It reports its own P(AI), its verdict, and the fields only it measures, and the result keeps all of it.

MemberWhat it readsWhat it reports

camerathe plain DINOv2 reading and the nine capture valuesthe camera head's P(AI) at 0.5 with a band of 0.04, and p_neutral, its score with the capture flags set

Routing is never by image size, and nothing routes at all now. The screenshot gate is a signal: the screenshot member reports it, and the combiner reads it to choose its anchor.

The revisions, in order

The revisions run after the stack, in a fixed order, because each reads the verdict the one before it left. Each records what it did.

#RevisionWhat it does
1combinerAnchors on the screenshot member when the gate fired and on the camera member otherwise. A valid, present capture signature leaves the anchor standing, capped at 0.10 under the screenshot anchor; otherwise the stack combiner of chapter 2 pools the anchor with the art, blur and human members.
2frame-seamWhen a real verdict hides a uniform dark frame, it runs the whole pipeline again on the interior and takes decisive interior evidence.
3preview-tailA small image read as generated with a clean semantic majority flips to real only when the preview member and the camera member's p_neutral both read it real, and only under the production profile.

The anchor rule is the old domain rule, written as a revision. On 2026-10-21 the restructure moved no number: the goldens, the battery and the census calls equal the two-lane detector's.

Stacking, not blending

Each revision either keeps the verdict or revises it on decisive evidence, and each gate was measured before it went live. The frame seam takes the interior's verdict only when it reads at least 0.70 and sits at least 0.20 above the outer score. The preview tail flips only under three agreeing reads.

The cost of a gate is a revision that rarely fires. The alternative, one model that blends every signal, raised real false positives from 8% to as much as 92% in the research's measurement.

What the result holds

The result is one JSON object with one verdict. Beside it sit every member's output, in the stack's order, and every revision's record, in theirs.

The result is schema v1, a clean break from schema v3 on 2026-10-21. Schema v3 named a domain and a base expert and kept a trace of the router's branches; the stack and the revisions are that trace now.

The keys are written sorted, so the same result is always the same bytes. A golden file can hold it, and the battery reads its verdict, the semantic tiles' grid and the capture member's present from it.

The goldens

Two crops hold the whole path. Their results are VeilGarde's own, written by veilgarde route --json, and the golden tests compare every later run with them: every label exact, every number within 1e-4.

For engineers: where each piece lives
  • DetectionSignals reads each signal at most once and caches it; Pipeline.run asks every StackMember, then hands the outputs to every Revision with a WorkingVerdict, and builds the InferenceResult.
  • Members live in Stack/Members, revisions in Revisions; ResultJSON writes the result, and RouteBatch runs a JSONL manifest through the same path.
  • The route golden tests hold this path to its own goldens and pin the two verdicts by value; rewrite the goldens with veilgarde route --json only when a change is meant to move them, and say so in the ledger.
